ThemeBulletin organizes its 65+ portfolio themes into four distinct categories, each built around a coherent visual and conceptual identity. Rather than offering a single generic design direction, the gallery is intentionally broad: you should be able to find a theme that feels like a genuine expression of your personality as a developer, not just a template you settled for. The four categories span technical minimalism, nostalgic web culture, cosmic creativity, and dark mysticism — and together they cover a wide range of professional personas.

## How Themes Are Organized in the Gallery

On the ThemeBulletin gallery homepage, the category overview board presents one sticky note per category. Each note shows the category name and the total number of themes it contains. Clicking a category note opens that category's theme board, where individual themes are displayed as polaroid photo cards paired with sticky notes, paginated 16 themes per board.

<Info>
  The Retro & Gaming category is the only category that currently spans more than one board (22 themes across 2 boards of 16). All other categories fit on a single board.
</Info>

## Choosing the Right Category

If you are unsure which category to start with, consider the impression you want to make on visitors to your portfolio:

- **Want to look sharp and technical?** Start with [Developer & Systems](/categories/developer-and-systems).
- **Want to stand out with strong personality and nostalgia?** Start with [Retro & Gaming](/categories/retro-and-gaming).
- **Want a visually striking, creative, or colorful identity?** Start with [Space & Creative](/categories/space-and-creative).
- **Want something dark, atmospheric, and unforgettable?** Start with [Spooky & Magical](/categories/spooky-and-magical).

<Tip>
  You are not limited to themes in one category. Open live demos from multiple categories side by side in different browser tabs to compare how each design actually feels before you commit to one.
</Tip>
